Orchestrated Objective Reduction

Orch OR, Honestly

A walkthrough of the Penrose–Hameroff theory in the narrowest reading that current data will bear — with every link in the causal chain labelled by what actually supports it.

Why this page exists. Most explanations of this theory present every step with the same confidence. In fact some links are measured, several are in vitro only, one has been experimentally excluded, and two are not testable with any instrument we have. Reading it as a chain of mixed-strength claims is the only way to see where the real work is.

04 — Anesthesia

What switching consciousness off should do

Anesthesia is the sharpest tool available, because it removes experience while leaving the stimulus and most of the machinery intact. The three live accounts predict different failures — which is what makes it worth running.

Note the overlap: the selection reading and ordinary classical access accounts predict the same outcome here. The experiment cleanly rules out content-generating Orch OR, and does not adjudicate between the other two. Only the dwell-time measurement carries information specific to a collapse threshold.

05 — The 2026 primate data

Where it fits the new result, and where it doesn't

Hesse and colleagues found that macaque face-patch populations alternate about fifteen times a second between representing a consciously seen face and a perceptually suppressed one, both at high fidelity, in the same neurons. It is the most relevant new evidence, and it cuts both ways.

What it helps

  • Candidates really are prepared in parallelTwo full representations coexist in one population, time-shared. The selection reading needs exactly this, and nobody had shown it.
  • Substrate-property theories are squeezedSuppressed content sits in the same cells, same linear code, near-equal fidelity. Theories where experience is a property of the substrate now owe an account of why one epoch is felt and the next is not.
  • Selection architectures gain groundAny theory where consciousness is a choice over prepared alternatives handles this naturally. Orch OR, read this way, is one.

What it doesn't

  • The discreteness isn't selectiveConscious and suppressed epochs alternate at the same grain. If each collapse is one conscious moment, the suppressed epochs need their own explanation — so the discreteness tracks a multiplexing schedule, not awareness.
  • 60 ms is not a distinctive signatureThe authors situate it among rhythmic attention, working-memory bursts and theta-paced hippocampal flickering. A prediction of "tens of milliseconds" is matched by a crowd of classical phenomena.
  • The content is thoroughly classicalBoth faces are reconstructable from firing rates. That is awkward for the version of Orch OR where quantum computation generates conscious content.
  • Crowded companyHigher-order theories and global workspace with dynamic routing explain the same data without a quantum mechanism.

06 — What would move the needle

Three tests, and what each can actually settle

No statistical test on neural data can detect non-computability — a non-computable sequence passes every randomness test by construction. The leverage is all in the physics of the collapse event, which makes a timing prediction nothing classical reproduces.
