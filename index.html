The Orch OR Chain

The Penrose–Hameroff theory drawn as a chain of mixed-strength claims, with every link labelled by what actually supports it.

Why the labels. Most explanations present every step with the same confidence. In fact some links are measured in living animals, several exist only in vitro, one has been excluded by a dedicated experiment, and two cannot be tested with any instrument now available. Reading it as a chain of unequal links is the only way to see where the real work is.

01 — The problem of scale

Fifteen orders of magnitude in time

The theory invokes physics at the femtosecond and behaviour at the second, and the argument lives in the gap between them. Select a marker.

02 — The problem of size

Four spatial scales, nested

Six orders of magnitude in space, from a cortical population down to a protein lattice. Each level makes a different claim, and they are not equally well supported.

03 — Mechanism

One conscious moment, step by step

The classical brain prepares the alternatives, a collapse event picks one, and the outcome is carried back out through ordinary signalling. Scrub through a single cycle.

05 — Anesthesia

What switching consciousness off should do

The sharpest tool available, because it removes experience while leaving the stimulus and most of the machinery intact. The three live accounts predict different failures.

Note the overlap. The selection reading and ordinary classical access accounts predict the same outcome here. The experiment cleanly rules out content-generating Orch OR and does not adjudicate between the other two. Only the dwell-time measurement carries information specific to a collapse threshold.

06 — The 2026 primate data

Where it fits the new result, and where it doesn't

Hesse and colleagues found macaque face-patch populations alternating about fifteen times a second between a consciously seen face and a perceptually suppressed one, both at high fidelity, in the same neurons. It cuts both ways.

What it helps

  • Candidates prepared in parallelTwo full representations coexist in one population, time-shared. The selection reading needs exactly this, and nobody had shown it.
  • Substrate theories squeezedSuppressed content sits in the same cells, same linear code, near-equal fidelity. Theories where experience is a property of the substrate now owe an account of why one epoch is felt and the next is not.
  • Selection architectures gainAny theory where consciousness is a choice over prepared alternatives handles this naturally. Orch OR, read this way, is one.

What it doesn't

  • Discreteness isn't selectiveConscious and suppressed epochs alternate at the same grain. If each collapse is one conscious moment, the suppressed epochs need their own explanation — so the discreteness tracks a multiplexing schedule, not awareness.
  • 60 ms is not distinctiveThe authors situate it among rhythmic attention, working-memory bursts and theta-paced hippocampal flickering. "Tens of milliseconds" is matched by a crowd of classical phenomena.
  • The content is classicalBoth faces reconstruct from firing rates. Awkward for the version where quantum computation generates conscious content.
  • Crowded companyHigher-order theories and global workspace with dynamic routing explain the same data without a quantum mechanism.
07 — What would move the needle

Three tests, and what each can settle

No statistical test on neural data can detect non-computability — a non-computable sequence passes every randomness test by construction. The leverage is in the physics of the collapse event, which makes a timing prediction nothing classical reproduces. FIG. 05 shows why.

On the reading. The selection framing — nothing informational held in superposition, the collapse event selecting among classically prepared alternatives — is a narrowing of the published theory, not its authors' own formulation. Where it departs from Hameroff and Penrose, the schedule in section 04 says so.

On the Platonic layer in FIG. 01. The tiling is a genuine Penrose P3 construction, and it is aperiodic: no translation carries it onto itself. It is not non-computable. It is computed here, by Robinson-triangle subdivision, and Penrose tilings can equally be generated by projection from a five-dimensional lattice — so this figure illustrates non-computability with an object an algorithm produced on the spot. What is actually true of the tiling, and what Penrose uses it for, is weaker: the matching rules are local, yet legal local patches exist that cannot be extended to cover the plane, so no greedy local procedure reaches a complete tiling. That is a claim about locality, not about computability. The undecidable result in this area — Berger's theorem on the general domino problem — concerns arbitrary tile sets, not these two rhombs, which provably do tile. Aperiodicity stands in for a stronger property no drawing can depict, and the Platonic layer should be read as iconography rather than as an argument.
